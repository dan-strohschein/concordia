# ADR-0077: Navigation: Recast/Detour navmeshes from static colliders, engine-native marks, per-agent crowds
- Status: Accepted (M2 phase 2.5a, 2026-11-22; author decision N1; updated in 2.5b.1 with N2 and N3, in 2.5c.1 with N5, and in 2.5c.2)
- Date: 2026-11-22
- Builds on: ADR-0063 (determinism contract), ADR-0069 (storage kinds), ADR-0072 (replay), ADR-0076 (Terra LOD), ADR-0067 (Godot mappings move to M5)

## Context
M2's navigation phase builds navmeshes with Recast, answers path queries with Detour, moves agents, runs crowds and lane traffic. The engine must stay port-neutral: the reference port's grid walkability lives in a private game, so tracked code can't name it. Navmeshes are derived data, and binary data may live only in `.concordia/ddc`. Simulation must be deterministic at any thread count (ADR-0063) and replayable from inputs (ADR-0072). And `unsafe` should be confined to as few crates as possible.

## Decision
1. **Vendored Recast and Detour, one safe crate.** `third_party/recastnavigation` is v1.6.0 (commit `6dc1667f`, zlib), with `Recast/` and `Detour/` only and unmodified, compiled with `DT_POLYREF64` and `-ffp-contract=off`. `recast-sys` holds a narrow C shim (`cr_*`), hand-written bindings checked by a header hash and struct sizes, counted allocations, **and the safe wrapper** (`build_tile`, `DtNavMesh`, `DtQuery`). It is the only navigation crate with `unsafe`; `concordia-nav` and later `concordia-terra-crowd` are `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`. This departs from `jolt-sys` and `ufbx-sys`, whose wrappers live in their consumers.
2. **Sources are what physics collides with.** A `NavMeshVolume` (an axis-aligned box on its entity, with agent size, cell size, tile size and contour error) builds from the static, non-sensor bodies physics accepts (`concordia_physics::static_solids`), tessellated from fixed 16-step tables (no trigonometry). Dynamic and kinematic bodies don't carve. Render meshes are not a source until physics has mesh colliders, so a level that characters can't walk on isn't walkable on the navmesh either.
3. **Engine-native marks.** `NavArea` (a box: `Blocked`, or `Cost { cost ≥ 1 }`) and `NavAreaGrid` (cells of `.` keep, `#` block, `o` walkable again, stored as text rows). They apply in order: areas by entity id, then grids by entity id. Upper bounds are exclusive (half-open cells). Blocked ground uses a temporary area id until all marks are applied, then becomes Recast's null area **before erosion**, so agents keep their radius from blocked cells as from walls. Distinct costs get area ids 2..=62 in ascending order. Game-specific walkability (the reference port's grid) maps onto `NavAreaGrid` in M5.
4. **Tiled, parallel, byte-identical builds.** The volume is cut into square tiles; each tile is built from the triangles and marks that overlap it plus Recast's border, independently and in parallel, and the tiles are kept in (z, x) order. The serialized navmesh (`CNAV` v1: the grid, search reach, costs and each tile's Detour data) is byte-identical for any thread count and any entity insertion order.
5. **Cached by input.** The cache key is a blake3 over a format tag, the Recast version, the settings, the volume bounds, the gathered triangles, the marks and the costs. The engine keeps a memo per world and volume and falls back to the DDC. A corrupt entry is rebuilt. `nav.build` and `nav.path` re-gather on every call, so they follow edits without an explicit rebuild.
6. **Play worlds.** At play start the nav module builds or loads every volume's navmesh into the `NavMeshes` resource. It isn't hashed, because it is derived from hashed state. Queries in processors read it (2.5b). Runtime rebuilds (moving obstacles) are a later step.
7. **Queries.** Nearest point (2 m sideways), path (Detour corridor plus string pulling; a partial corridor ends at the closest reachable point, which Detour clamps), and surface raycast. A pool of Detour query objects makes a navmesh safe to query from many threads with identical results.
8. **Debug layers.** `EngineModule` names debug layers and supplies world-space lines and triangles; `capture.screenshot`'s `debug` and `viewport.overlays`' `debug` select them, and the renderer projects them under the editor overlays. Nav draws `navmesh` (polygons by island, outlined). Crowds and traffic add their own layers without touching the renderer.
9. **Crowds are per-agent Terra processors (N1).** Crowd state (corridor, velocity, neighbours, target) lives in hashed components. Detour's crowd steps are rewritten in Rust as processors over Detour queries, following DetourCrowd's zlib source: path requests (budgeted per tick in entity order), corridor update, neighbours from a deterministic grid, steering, sampled velocity-obstacle avoidance, integration on the surface, and collision resolution from last-tick state. They run in the parallel scheduler and opt into simulation LOD. `dtCrowd` is not wrapped (2.5c).

## Alternatives
- **Wrap `dtCrowd` in one processor.** Faster to build, but its state would sit outside the world: not hashed or inspectable, single-threaded and untouched by LOD. The author chose per-agent processors (N1).
- **Single-threaded nav and crowds in a `Sim` set** (the old plan). It's superseded by Terra's deterministic parallel scheduler; `Sim` became `PostPhysics`.
- **Render meshes as navmesh sources.** They would make walkable ground characters can't stand on.
- **Recast's `rcMarkBoxArea` as is.** Its inclusive upper bound marks one cell too many on aligned grids, and it can't revive blocked ground.
- **Erode, then mark** (RecastDemo's order). Agents would brush blocked cells by their radius.
- **Recast's default contour error (1.3 cells) only.** It is the default, but `edge_max_error` is exposed: below 0.7 cells, outlines are exact to the cell, which hand-checked tests and grid-shaped levels need.
- **A `game-level` crate supplying marks.** Tracked code must stay port-neutral (rule: never copy port details); engine components are the boundary.

## Consequences
- New crates `recast-sys` and `concordia-nav`; `[nav]` in `concordia.toml` (written by `concordia new` and added by `concordia studio init`); methods `nav.build` and `nav.path` (MCP `nav_build`, `nav_path`); the level designer gets both and the `concordia-navigation` skill.
- `capture.screenshot` gains `debug`, and `Overlays` gains `debug` (editor schemas regenerated).
- Recast rules to know: a span is at least one cell tall, so a floor's walkable surface sits up to one `cell_height` above it; a face exactly on a cell boundary belongs to one side only; erosion keeps the cell diagonal to a blocked corner (Recast's distance transform). Detour's A* runs over portal midpoints, so paths across tile seams can bend at a seam.
- Cross-platform byte equality depends on glam's arithmetic paths and Recast's floats with `-ffp-contract=off`; equality across thread counts and insertion orders is tested.
- M5 maps `NavigationAgent3D`/`NavigationRegion3D` and the port's grid onto these components (semantics only, from Godot's MIT docs). M7 7.7 keeps `crowd_of_10k_agents_avoids_overlap` and crowd-scale extraction.

## Update (M2 phase 2.5b.1, 2026-11-22): agents
- **`NavAgent` is gameplay's request; `NavAgentState` is the engine's.** `NavAgent` (target, max speed, radius, height, path and target desired distances, path max distance, an optional volume) follows Godot's `NavigationAgent3D` semantics (MIT docs; M5 maps onto it) and is written only by gameplay. `NavAgentState` (status, the target it planned for, the string-pulled waypoints, the next corner, partial, volume, the tick it started waiting) is hashed and written only by nav's processors, in N1's spirit. The polygon corridor is 2.5c's crowd state.
- **Two processors in `PrePhysics`.** `nav.plan` reads the `NavMeshes` resource and plans the agents that need it, at most `[nav] plans_per_tick` (default 32) a tick in `(waiting_since, id)` order, so nobody starves. `nav.follow` advances corners, checks arrival and writes `CharacterMotion`, slowed on the last stretch so no tick passes the end. Physics moves the body the same tick. An entity's `NavAgent` owns its `CharacterMotion`.
- **Distances are sideways**, within half the agent's height vertically (the navmesh surface sits up to one `cell_height` above the floor). The agent turns within `path_desired_distance` of a corner, so its track stays within that distance of the path.
- **Replanning** happens when the target moves farther than `target_desired_distance`, or when the agent is more than `path_max_distance` off its current segment. There's no timer until navmeshes change at runtime. A failed target is retried only when the target moves.
- **Signals:** `NavArrived { target, position, partial }` (an unreachable target walks to the closest point and arrives partial), and `NavPathFailed { target, reason, detail }` once per target. Both are raised on the agent and delivered next tick (ADR-0069), whatever its LOD (ADR-0076).
- **No LOD opt-in.** Following costs a few vector operations, physics moves every character every tick anyway, and a skipped follower would overshoot by holding its velocity. Planning is budgeted instead; 2.5c's steering and avoidance opt in.
- **N3. Sizes:** the controller is the body and always collides. An agent whose radius or height differs from its controller's, or whose radius is wider than its navmesh's `agent_radius`, warns once per agent and volume and still moves.
- **N2. Click-to-move (2.5b.2):** the pointer becomes a world-space ray in input, recorded by replays, so simulation never depends on the window or the renderer. An engine processor turns the ray into a target (physics raycast, then `nearest_point`).
- Scripts set targets by writing `NavAgent` and receive the signals by name, mirroring the types field for field (ADR-0062); no new import. The `nav_paths` debug layer draws each walking agent's remaining path.

## Update (M2 phase 2.5c.1, 2026-11-22): crowds
- **A crowd agent is a `NavAgent` plus a `CrowdAgent`.** `CrowdAgent` (DetourCrowd's agent parameters with its sample's defaults, and `avoidance: Option<CrowdAvoidance>` holding the sampling parameters) is gameplay's request that the crowd steer this agent. It lives in `concordia-nav`, which must know which agents it leaves to the crowd. The crowd's work (`CrowdState`, the `CrowdGrid` snapshot, the `crowd.*` processors, the `Crowd` trait and the `crowd` layer) is `concordia-terra-crowd`, enabled with `[nav]`.
- **Planning stays nav's.** Crowd agents share `nav.plan`'s queue and budget. `NavAgentState.corridor` holds their polygon corridor (empty for plain agents; Detour references are stable because the navmesh is byte-identical). They need no controller, are placed on their polygon while idle, and don't replan for being off their path: the crowd sets `Waiting` when it loses the corridor. `nav.follow` and `nav_paths` skip them (`View::query_filtered`, a new Terra filter).
- **N5. Two bodies, picked per entity.** Without a `CharacterController`, a crowd agent is a *surface* agent: the crowd moves its `Transform` along the navmesh (`moveAlongSurface`, `getPolyHeight`) and physics never sees it. With one, it is a *physics* agent: the crowd's velocity, plus its collision push as a velocity, goes to `CharacterMotion`. Every `CharacterController` is solid to surface agents: they yield and never push it. Jolt characters don't collide with each other, so the crowd keeps physics agents apart.
- **The chain** is DetourCrowd's `dtCrowd::update`, split into processors in `PrePhysics` after `nav.plan`, each writing only its own agent: grid, corridor (bodies followed, corners, the visibility shortcut), neighbours (all in range, nearest 6 by (distance², id)), steer (smooth or straight, the end slow-down, separation), integrate (accelerating toward the chosen velocity; a step never passes the end), collide (4 Jacobi iterations over the previous iteration's candidates), and move (surface moves, physics velocities, arrival). Others are read only from snapshots built in id order, so the hash is the same at any thread count.
- **Shim:** `cr_move_along_surface`, `cr_poly_height` and `cr_raycast_path`, each narrow. The corridor helpers (`dtMergeCorridorStartMoved`, `dtMergeCorridorStartShortcut`, `findCorners`' pruning) are rewritten in Rust. Sliced pathfinding, topology optimization, off-mesh connections and validity checks aren't needed (navmeshes don't change at runtime).
- **Not yet:** sampled velocity-obstacle avoidance, the local wall boundary and the LOD opt-in are 2.5c.2. The crowd's per-agent processors are declared LOD-aware already, so far agents will choose velocities less often, while the grid, neighbours, integration, collision and movement stay at full rate.

## Update (M2 phase 2.5c.2, 2026-11-22): avoidance and LOD
- **`crowd.avoid`** (between steer and integrate) is DetourCrowd's `sampleVelocityAdaptive`, rewritten as pure functions:
  - The obstacles are the agent's neighbours (circles with last tick's snapshot position, velocity and desired velocity; characters included) and the walls it faces (segments).
  - The penalties, early-outs and pattern follow DetourCrowd exactly, with every candidate tried in a fixed order (ties keep the first).
  - Cos and sin of 2π/n and π/n come from a fixed table, not run-time trigonometry, so results are the same wherever the arithmetic is IEEE.
  - With `avoidance: None`, the chosen velocity is the desired one.
  - **Two deliberate differences from DetourCrowd:**
    - an even `divs` fills the ring slot DetourCrowd leaves stale;
    - neighbours' desired velocities are last tick's (DetourCrowd's are the same tick's), so no agent sees another's half-finished step.
- **The local boundary** (`dtLocalBoundary`) is hashed state: `CrowdState.boundary_center` and at most 8 `walls`.
  - It is gathered by two new narrow shim queries, `cr_local_neighbourhood` and `cr_poly_wall_segments`, when there is no boundary yet or the agent has moved over a quarter of its query range.
  - The validity re-check is left out, because navmeshes don't change at runtime.
- **Simulation LOD:** `crowd.corridor`, `crowd.steer` and `crowd.avoid` are `.lod()`. A far agent re-picks its corners, desired velocity, walls and chosen velocity only when due, and holds them in between. The grid, neighbours, integration, collision, movement and arrival stay full-rate, so far agents still collide, stay on the navmesh and never pass their end.
- **The query pool is sharded:** `NavMesh` keeps 16 pools of Detour query objects, picked by thread. One shared mutex made the crowd's queries slower at 8 threads than at one. Results can't change, because every Detour call resets its object.
- **Measured:** `crowd_10k` (one tick of 10,000 surface agents) takes 12.3 ms plain and 12.4 ms with avoidance and LOD at 8 threads (`concordia-terra-crowd/benches/BASELINE.md`). M7 7.7 keeps the 100k target and the perf test.

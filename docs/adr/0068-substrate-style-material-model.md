# ADR-0068: Substrate-style material model
- Status: Accepted (M2 phase 2.3a, 2026-11-20); the renderer in 2.3b (2026-11-20); migration, editor and Studio in 2.3c (2026-11-20); a pipeline variant per program shape (2.3c follow-ups, 2026-11-21); FBX import, materials as one slab like glTF (2.3d, 2026-11-21)
- Date: 2026-11-20
- Builds on: ADR-0009 (canonical RON), ADR-0040 (renderer), ADR-0044 (assets), ADR-0067 decision D5 (materials follow Substrate, text first)

## Context
M1 has one metallic-roughness material (glTF 2.0 core). D5 replaces it with a model in the style of Unreal's Substrate: slabs with BSDF features, combined by layering, mixing and coverage operators, under a per-pixel closure budget. It is defined as text first; node-graph authoring waits for M4's graphs. Clean room: built only from Epic's public Substrate documentation and the SIGGRAPH 2023 Substrate course, plus the papers named per lobe, never Unreal source.

## Decision
1. **Format: a flat map of named nodes.** A `.material.ron` holds `nodes` (name → `Slab(..)`, `Vertical(top, bottom)`, `Horizontal(a, b, mix, mix_texture)` or `Coverage(node, coverage)`), a `root`, and `alpha_mode`, `double_sided`, `unlit`. Nodes refer to each other by name. The reflection system has no recursive types, and a named graph is what M4's node graphs will produce. Names are `[A-Za-z0-9_-]+`, references must exist, cycles are errors naming the path, every node must be reachable from the root, and a node may be used twice. The text is canonical through `concordia-scene`'s document writer.
2. **Slab.** It has M1's fields (base color with alpha as opacity, metallic, roughness, normal, occlusion and emission, with their textures), plus `specular` (dielectric F0 = 0.08 × specular; 0.5 is M1's 0.04), `transmittance` (light passing the medium at normal incidence; it matters only under a layer above) and four optional features:
   - **haze:** a second GGX lobe with its own roughness, mixed by weight, sharing the slab's F0;
   - **fuzz:** a Charlie sheen with the Estevez–Kulla Λ visibility;
   - **subsurface:** a per-channel mean free path (metres, ≤ 0.1). Forward-only approximation: energy-conserving wrapped diffuse, w = mfp / (mfp + 1 cm), so light reaches the surface from below its horizon. Screen-space diffusion waits for M4;
   - **thin film:** thickness in nm and film IOR. Airy reflectance of one dielectric film over a base whose IOR comes from F0 per channel, sampled at 630, 532 and 465 nm, replacing the slab's Fresnel.
3. **Energy conservation, exactly.**
   - Diffuse is scaled by 1 − E_spec(N·V), the directional albedo of the slab's own specular lobes from a table of the direct lobe (GGX with height-correlated Smith, the same terms the BSDF uses).
   - Sheen is divided by max(1, E_sheen) and scales the rest by 1 − max(color)·E_sheen. The sheen table goes in the albedo-table texture in 2.3b.
   - M1's coupling (diffuse × (1 − F(V·H))) gains energy at grazing views: up to 1.107 for a white, full-specular dielectric. The author chose exact conservation over pixel parity, so migrated M1 materials change slightly (about 1–2% of their diffuse head-on, more at silhouettes). Goldens that moved beyond tolerance were reviewed and re-blessed in 2.3b; 2.3c moved none.
4. **Operators** (what each passes up):
   - **Vertical:** color = top + T_top(v)·T_top(l)·bottom; emission = top + T_top(v)·bottom; alpha = 1 − (1 − a_top)(1 − a_bottom). A slab's transmittance toward a direction at cosine μ is (1 − E_spec(μ)) × transmittance^(1/μ) × (1 − sheen).
   - **Horizontal:** a linear mix of everything.
   - **Coverage:** like mixing with nothing (no light, full transmittance).
   - Slab alpha is coverage only in `Blend` materials (premultiplied results); otherwise it feeds only the mask test, as in M1.
5. **Closures and programs.** `concordia-material` compiles a material into a postfix program: one `Slab` op per closure (per slab use) and one op per operator, evaluated on a small stack. The GPU (2.3b) interprets the same program, so CPU and GPU share one definition.
6. **Closure budget: platform default, lowerable** (author's choice). Each platform has a maximum (1..=8), and a project may lower it in `concordia.toml` `[render] closure_budget` but never raise it. Metal (the M3 Pro) defaults to **8** (author's choice in 2.3b: nothing is simplified on the reference hardware; projects lower it for weaker targets). A value outside 1..=8 is refused when the project loads; one above a lower platform's default is ignored with a warning. A material over budget is downgraded by parameter blending (Substrate's documented fallback), deterministically:
   - first the first mergeable horizontal mix in post-order, then the first vertical layer;
   - a mix becomes the parameter blend of its two slabs at its mix;
   - a layer tints the bottom by the top's normal-incidence transmittance squared, blends toward the top by the top's opaque share, and keeps the top's highlight as haze (weight from the F0 ratio);
   - textures and features that cannot be kept are reported as `node.field`.
   `fit_budget` returns the fitted material and a `ClosureReport` (budget, before, after, merges); the renderer fits each material at upload, keeps the program until the material or budget changes, logs a downgrade once and lists it in `render.stats` (`closure_budget`, `downgraded_materials`) for every frame that draws it.
7. **Migration.** `read_material` accepts M1's flat text and returns one slab named `surface` with the same values; `read_material_with_format` also says which format it read. Since 2.3b the material importer, `AssetDb::load_material`, the built-in default material and the renderer use `Material`, and the glTF importer stores each material as one slab (a material artifact has one format). In 2.3c `MaterialData` was retired: M1's shape is read only by a private type with M1's defaults, nothing writes it, and the glTF importer builds `Slab` directly. Every M1 golden is drawn from M1 text through `read_material`, unchanged from the 2.3b baseline.
   - **Editing (2.3c):** `material.get` / `material.set` read and write the canonical text (file writes attributed in `log.tail`, not undoable, like `asset.import`), refuse invalid materials naming the `node.field`, and import what they write. Each import that finds new content bumps the asset's generation, and the renderer re-sends a material whose generation moved, so the next frame draws it. Saving a migrated M1 file rewrites it as layered text.
8. **Crates.** The model and format live in `concordia-assets` (which owns `AssetRef` and `.material.ron`). Evaluation, the budget, the albedo tables and sampling live in the new `concordia-material`, which doesn't depend on the renderer, so M4's path tracer can use it as its BSDF. The split-sum IBL table moved there from `concordia-render` (which re-exports it).
9. **On the GPU (2.3b).** `forward.slang` interprets the same program and mirrors the CPU reference term for term:
   - per light, the whole program is evaluated for that light's direction (so layering uses each light's own transmittance);
   - image-based lighting runs the program once more, each closure lit by the environment (split-sum specular per lobe, haze blended, thin film replacing F0 at N·V; SH diffuse × (1 − E_spec(N·V)), the same energy split as direct light; sheen as fuzz × E_sheen × the prefiltered environment at the sheen roughness), with the light-side transmittance taken toward the mirror direction, whose cosine is the view's;
   - unlit materials and the albedo view show `MaterialProgram::unlit`: each slab's albedo (dielectric medium share, or a metal's base color) and emission, combined by the operators at normal incidence;
   - the split-sum, direct-lobe and sheen tables share one texture (two 32×32 halves) read at texel centers, exactly like the CPU tables; it is uploaded with or without an environment;
   - a mix texture's R multiplies `mix`; textures multiply factors as in M1.
   - the mask test compares the program's alpha (slab alphas combined by the operators) before anything is shaded; view-only terms are computed once per closure, and light-side transmittance only for programs with a vertical layer.
   - **A pipeline variant per program shape** (2.3c follow-ups). A program's shape is its op kinds, which closure each slab op pushes, each closure's features, and whether it has a vertical layer. A material with more than one op draws with `forward.slang` compiled for its shape (preprocessor constants, the same source). The interpreter unrolls, so its stack, closures and feature branches resolve at compile time and stay in registers. Values (colors, mixes, textures) stay in the material rows, so materials of one shape share a variant. One-slab materials keep the base shader. A variant compiles once per shape, about 0.2 s plus Metal's compile, and the shader cache keeps it across runs. Under 1,024 lights a clear coat went from about 8× one slab to 1.6×, and three closures from 18× to 2.5×.

## Alternatives
- **A recursive operator tree in the file.** It is natural to write, but `concordia-reflect` has no `Box`, and agents editing deep nesting make more mistakes than with named nodes.
- **Generating shader code per material.** This is what Substrate does with node graphs, but it means a pipeline per material and a compile on every edit. One interpreted program kept M1's single pipeline in 2.3b; since the 2.3c follow-ups the same interpreter is specialized per shape, not per material, so editing values never recompiles. Code generation can come with M4's graphs.
- **Keeping M1's diffuse coupling** for exact golden parity: rejected by the author in favor of exact energy conservation.
- **A fixed closure budget, or a free project setting:** the author chose platform default plus lowering.
- **Neubelt's sheen visibility:** simpler, but its albedo reaches 1.7 at grazing views for low roughness. Even Λ needs the albedo table to stay ≤ 1.

## Consequences
- Every BSDF change is tested against independent reference values and white-furnace bounds (including a deterministic grid of bright extremes, because random cases rarely reach them), and checked by mutation.
- The renderer must mirror `eval_slab` and the program interpreter. 2.3b adds a test comparing shader output to the CPU reference per feature, besides the goldens.
- Subsurface is an approximation until M4's screen-space work. Thin film samples three wavelengths, so very thick films alias.
- **Goldens moved by the energy rule (measured in 2.3b, FLIP mean, tolerance in brackets):** `shadow_box_plane_iso` 0.032 [0.01], `view_mode_lit` 0.015 [0.01], `view_mode_shadow_cascades` 0.014 [0.01], `many_lights_iso` 0.012 [0.01] and `pbr_sphere_grid_directional` 0.010 [0.01] crossed their tolerance; `gltf_metal_rough_spheres(_no_textures)` 0.016–0.018 [0.02], the other glTF goldens 0.004–0.008 [0.02], `orthographic_isometric` 0.003, `light_clusters` 0.003, `spot_light_cone` 0.003 and `pbr_spheres_ibl` 0.002 moved within it. Data views (unlit, albedo, normals, roughness/metallic, overdraw, wireframe) are byte-identical. With M1's coupling put back temporarily, every golden returns to M1 (max one 8-bit step; IBL spheres FLIP 0.0002 from the wider table texture), so the movement is the energy rule alone.
